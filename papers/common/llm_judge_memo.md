# Common Reading Memo: LLM-as-a-Judge Survey (Gu et al., 2024–2025)

**Paper:** A Survey on LLM-as-a-Judge (Gu et al., 2024–2025, latest revision)
**Role:** Judge-design reference for `scoring_evaluator.py` and `judge_filter.py`

---

## Key Contributions

Gu et al. survey 150+ papers on LLM-as-a-judge and consolidate failure modes and design principles. Four findings are directly applicable to this project:

1. **Positional and verbosity bias.** LLM judges systematically favour longer responses and responses in the first position in pairwise comparisons. Pointwise scoring (scoring each response independently against a rubric) avoids positional bias entirely.

2. **Calibration requires explicit anchors.** Judges asked to score on a 1–5 scale produce inconsistent scores unless the rubric provides concrete examples for each score level. Without anchors, different judges (or the same judge at different temperatures) produce different distributions.

3. **Self-consistency as a reliability signal.** Running the same judge prompt twice at temperature=0 and checking for agreement is a cheap reliability check. Agreement rate > 95% is the threshold Gu et al. recommend before treating LLM judge scores as ground truth.

4. **Model family separation.** The model that generates training data should not be the model that judges it. Self-judging inflates scores and masks failure modes the generator is predisposed to produce.

---

## Application to Tenacious-Bench

### `judge_filter.py` — pointwise scoring

The judge filter uses pointwise scoring (three independent dimensions, each 1–5) rather than pairwise ranking. This directly implements Gu et al.'s recommendation to avoid positional bias. The judge model (Claude Haiku 4.5) is distinct from the generation models (DeepSeek V3, programmatic templates), satisfying the model family separation requirement.

The judge prompt is externalized in `generation/prompts/judge_system_prompt.md` and requires output in the format `SCORES: input_coherence=N, ground_truth_verifiability=N, rubric_clarity=N`. The structured output format reduces parsing ambiguity and is consistent with Gu et al.'s recommendation to constrain judge output format.

### `scoring_evaluator.py` — score anchors

The RUBRIC CALIBRATION comment block in `scoring_evaluator.py` provides concrete anchors for the aggregate score:
- 5.0 = all constraints met, specific signal referenced, zero banned phrases
- 3.5 = minor miss, overall on-brand
- 2.0 = meaningful failure on ≥1 dimension
- 1.0 = multiple categorical failures

This directly implements Gu et al.'s calibration requirement. Without these anchors, the weighted composite score would be difficult to interpret across evaluators.

### IRA as a self-consistency check

Gu et al.'s self-consistency signal maps directly to the inter-rater agreement protocol in `dataset/inter_rater_agreement.md`. Rather than running the same judge twice, we ran two human annotators against the LLM judge. Round 1 found κ = 0.662 on `tone_checker_fn` — below the 0.70 reliability threshold. Root cause: the mock heuristic gave partial credit to tier-1 brand violations. Revised to a two-tier system; Round 2 achieved κ = 1.000. This is exactly the calibration loop Gu et al. recommend.

### One Gu et al. risk not yet mitigated

Gu et al. flag **sycophancy bias**: LLM judges rate outputs from the same model family higher than outputs from other families. Our judge (Claude Haiku) judges tasks generated by Claude Sonnet (12 adversarial tasks). This is a within-family pair — Haiku may rate Sonnet-generated tasks higher than DeepSeek-generated tasks. Mitigation: report judge scores stratified by `generation_model` in the final results.
